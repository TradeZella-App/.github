## No Installer, and Why That Is the Whole Answer

No. TradeZella publishes no `.exe`, no `.dmg` and no store listing. It is a browser application served from
app.tradezella.com, and the site never links to an installer — every call to action reads "Get Started" and
opens a signup form, not a file. Mobile is the same story: an App Store search for TradeZella
returns other developers' journals, and the site advertises no iOS or Android build.

That is a design choice. Pulling fills from 500+ integrations, running strategies across 11+ years of history
and rendering one-second charts happen server-side, so a local binary would mostly be a window on a remote
workspace.

The desktop experience most traders settle on is a browser-installed web app: Chrome and Edge offer "Install
this site as an app", Safari offers "Add to Dock", and either drops a real icon that launches TradeZella in a
chromeless window. It behaves like an installed program — it just isn't compiled as one.

## Connecting a Broker: Read-Only, Zero Custody

TradeZella's own FAQ says it plainly — the company is not a brokerage; you execute at your broker and the data
transfers in afterwards. That settles custody: TradeZella never holds cash or positions, cannot place, modify
or close an order, and offers no wallet, deposit address or withdrawal flow.

What an integration grants is history. Connecting Interactive Brokers, Tradovate, NinjaTrader, MetaTrader 4/5,
thinkorswim, Webull, Robinhood, eToro, cTrader, OANDA, Tastytrade, Rithmic, Bybit or Coinbase hands over a read
of executed fills — instrument, direction, size, entry, exit, timestamps, commissions — so the journal writes
itself. Futures and crypto are first-class here, and PropFirm Sync tracks FTMO, Topstep, Apex and a dozen more
evaluation accounts on one dashboard.

Treat that access like any credential. Where a broker supports a scoped read-only API key, use one and leave
trading and withdrawal permissions off; where it wants full login details, weigh that against the CSV upload
route kept for unsupported brokers.

## Backtesting and Replay, Beyond a Spreadsheet

Manual backtesting steps a chart forward bar by bar across more than a decade of history at one-second
resolution, with position sizing, indicators, multiple timeframes, an economic calendar and a journal attached
to the session — future price stays hidden until you advance it, which is the entire point. Sessions are
unlimited and kept indefinitely on every tier, across stocks, futures, crypto and CFDs.

Trade Replay is the mirror image, aimed at trades you actually took: a real fill played back second by second
with entries and exits marked, adjustable speed, and Time &amp; Sales plus Level II data alongside. Watching an
execution at the speed it happened surfaces what a P&amp;L column never will — the stop moved after the fact, the
size that was wrong before the idea was. On Pro and Ultra, Zella AI adds commentary.
